Research agenda

Transportation markets as systems of strategic response.

The central question is not simply which transport solution is optimal. It is what firms and institutions choose when policy, competition and market conditions change—and why those endogenous choices make the realised equilibrium depart from a single-agent optimum.

Research programme

Three connected bodies of work

I

Competition under network and capacity constraints

Air–rail competition and cooperation, airline network choice, airport congestion, service frequency and on-time performance.

  • Networks are strategic objects, not background conditions.
  • Local competition must be evaluated in a system-wide equilibrium.
II

Coordination across the transport decision chain

Airline partnerships, airport–carrier interactions, regional-service contracts, port systems, fuel supply, finance and infrastructure investment.

  • Individually optimal contracts may not be system-optimal.
  • Institutional design matters when benefits and risks are distributed.
III

Strategic adaptation under policy intervention

SAF subsidies and mandates, CORSIA, e-fuels, carbon policy, green technology adoption and unintended market responses.

  • Policy effects depend on competition and market expansion.
  • Flexibility can improve adoption—or create leakage and avoidance.

Methodological development

Four ways the decision frame expands

01

Decision variables

Beyond price and traffic volume to frequency, fleet, network structure, market coverage, service quality, assets, technology, fuels, contracts and investment.

02

Strategic decision chain

Beyond operators and infrastructure to fuel suppliers, freight forwarders, financiers, platforms, local governments and regulators.

03

Dynamic environment

Ambiguity, irreversibility, the option to wait, long-term commitment, learning, path dependence and state-dependent policy.

04

Policy transmission

From a policy shock through multidimensional responses and actor interaction to equilibrium reconstruction and system performance.

Methods & evidence

Competition, network choice, contracts and bargaining.

Dynamic models & mechanism design

Entry timing, learning, financing and policy commitment.

Causal identification

Policy variation, shocks and high-frequency operational data.

Structural & counterfactual analysis

Quantifying frictions and comparing policy mechanisms.

Next frontier

Moving from observed response to dynamic policy design—and to the endogenous formation of emerging mobility markets.

01

Micro-identification

Link planned decisions, actual execution, interaction sequences and cross-market outcomes using operational, network and asset-level data.

02

Adaptive institutions

Design credible commitments, policy portfolios and coordination mechanisms that anticipate waiting, avoidance and learning.

03

Emerging markets

Study how entry, infrastructure, access rules and multimodal competition jointly form low-altitude and other new transport markets.
