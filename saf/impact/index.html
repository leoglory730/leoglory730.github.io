Three levels of demonstrated impact

Documented influence, not output alone.

The strongest evidence is where an external source does something identifiable with the work: maps its intellectual position, uses a result in scenario design or carries aviation evidence into formal policy analysis.

Research into decision value

Three questions the programme can now answer better.

These are policy-relevant contributions rather than claims of regulatory adoption. Each case links a research result to the decision it can inform.

01

How should a quota allow flexibility?

Airport-level flexibility and book-and-claim can improve efficiency, but safeguards are needed because gains and burdens may be distributed unevenly across major, small and remote airports.

Decision value

Gives regulators a way to judge flexibility by both system efficiency and distributional consequences.

Transportation Research Part B · 2026 ↗
02

Which SAF pathway fits which region?

Four pathway-specific indices distinguish HEFA, Fischer–Tropsch, alcohol-to-jet and power-to-liquid potential using resources, infrastructure, energy systems and market demand.

Decision value

Replaces a single technology ranking with a replicable framework for region-specific investment and policy choices.

Transportation Research Part A · 2026 ↗
03

How can airports accelerate adoption?

A connected set of studies compares subsidy mechanisms and shows how quota design changes the case for airport fuel-facility ownership.

Decision value

Connects climate ambition with the operating, governance and cost-sharing decisions that determine whether SAF can be delivered at airports.

Two Transportation Research Part A studies · 2025 ↗

Why this matters for Asia

Transferable rules, not one-size-fits-all prescriptions.

Asia’s aviation markets differ sharply in feedstock, renewable energy, airport geography, airline structure, infrastructure and policy capacity. The programme is designed around those differences.

Markets

Different competitive effects

Mandates can affect full-service and low-cost carriers differently, so distribution and market structure belong inside policy design.

Places

Different production strengths

Regional pathway indices make resource, industrial and energy-system differences visible before technology or investment choices are made.

Institutions

Different delivery models

Airport subsidies, fuel-facility ownership and international carbon rules shape who invests, who bears risk and how adoption proceeds.

Verification

An evidence register judges can follow.

Independent studies and institutional documents are distinguished from publication records, self-reported data and professional communication.

Responsible claim boundary

The evidence demonstrates scholarly uptake, agenda influence, formal analytical use, peer recognition and decision-facing reach. It does not yet prove that a government, airport or airline changed a specific decision because of the work.

The programme is therefore described as moving from publication to intellectual influence, cross-sector dialogue and decision support—not as having already transformed regulation or SAF deployment.
