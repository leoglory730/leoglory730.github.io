The research

One transition.
Three connected questions.

SAF is a near-term route to lower-carbon aviation, but high costs, uneven capacity and fragmented rules can make well-intended policy backfire. The programme replaces one-size-fits-all prescriptions with decision rules for governments, airports and airlines.

01

Policy design

Testing mandates, subsidies, CORSIA incentives and flexible compliance so that regulation reduces emissions without creating avoidable market distortions.

02

Market architecture

Examining airline competition, airport incentives and fuel-facility ownership to connect climate ambition with implementable operating models.

03

Regional pathways

Using a data-driven SAF Development Index to match HEFA, Fischer–Tropsch, alcohol-to-jet and power-to-liquid pathways to regional strengths.

Core proposition
The best SAF policy is the rule that aligns costs, infrastructure and incentives well enough to accelerate real adoption.

Research into exchange

A bilateral route to implementation.

The programme works with a wider network that includes the Australia–China Cooperation for Sustainable Aviation project, the Green Aviation Research Centre at Beihang University, PolyU colleagues and other academic and industry partners. The collaboration supports the work; it does not define it.
